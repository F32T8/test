I sometimes stray from this, so consider it aspirational. 01. Visit Websites Seems obvious, but the big platforms heavily discourage this activity. There’s a wide world to be found outside of the walled gardens. A good place to start is the Links page of your current favorite website. There are also a lot of great webrings that offer near-endless exploration, such as webring.xxiivv.com. 02. Use Bookmarks Need a handy way to return to those cool sites you’ve been visiting? Use your browser’s Bookmarks feature. It works much better than memory alone. Make it a habit to check in on your favorite sites on a regular basis. This is a great alternative to doomscrolling social media. 03. Tell Your Friends If you have a website, make a Links page to highlight your favorite places to visit. Find something someone you know may be interested in? Let ‘em know. Even less-online normies get a kick out of visiting websites. It’s a rare treat that almost everyone appreciates. Sharing is caring. 04. Subscribe to RSS Want all the content to come to you, instead? Subscribe to RSS feeds. This is an underrated, but incredibly handy utility. And it comes in clutch when you’re on the go. Feeling the urge to look at your phone? Checking in on your RSS subscriptions is much more rewarding than opening any social media app. And there are lots of open source options, too. My personal favorite is Feeder, if you happen to be an Android user. 05. Make and Share Stuff If you are attracted to this sort of online experience, why not participate? Making a website is easier than they’d have you believe. And your valuable contribution will help ensure an active and lively cyberspace for years to come. Need help getting a site up and running? There are lots of options out there. But feel free to shoot me an email with any questions or straight up steal my website’s code. 06. Send Emails Social media sucks. But we can still have a social internet. There are lots of alternative options, but email is tried and true and underrated. Most personal websites list this bit of contact info. And if they do, that almost certainly means they’re interested in receiving emails. Like something you see or just want to respond? Take a chance and send an email. You might even make a new friend. 07. Moderate Intake All things in moderation, as they say. Recreating an infinite scroll should not be your goal. Instead, what you want to do is engage only with things you care about or are interested in. And when all the new stuff is consumed, learn to feel full and do other things. Touch grass on occasion. Engage with other types of media. Variety is the spice of life. — — — — — — # ABOUT age:36 | pro:he/him | loc:usa | bld:a+ FREECIGS.org is a semi-anonymous journal and a dumping ground for ideas, lists, and other texts. There's a bit of HTML/CSS to wrap the text, make the line height a little more comfortable, and allow for dark mode. The goal is to keep things as simple as possible and to work within restraints. Largely inspired by textonly.website (which is maintained by koray.er), minim.blog, divacorp.net, and the idea of keeping a digital journal using a .txt file. Lastly, this site should not be discoverable via search engines and has a robots.txt file that blocks (or discourages) crawlers.
